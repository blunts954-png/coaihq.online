========================================
COMMON CALIFORNIA TENANT LAW ISSUES
========================================

1. SECURITY DEPOSIT DISPUTES (§ 1950.7)
   - Landlord must return deposit within 21 days
   - Must provide itemized deduction statement
   - Can only deduct for damage beyond normal wear and tear
   - Violation = up to 2x deposit as penalty
   - STRONG case if: landlord missed 21-day deadline, no itemization, tenant has photos

2. EVICTION WITHOUT PROPER NOTICE (§ 1940-1954.1)
   - 30-day notice required for month-to-month (if tenant <1 year)
   - 60-day notice if tenant >1 year
   - 3-day notice only for: non-payment, lease violation, nuisance
   - STRONG case if: no proper notice given, retaliation suspected

3. UNINHABITABLE CONDITIONS (§ 1941)
   - Landlord must maintain: plumbing, heating, weatherproofing, sanitation
   - Tenant can withhold rent if uninhabitable
   - Must notify landlord in writing first
   - MODERATE case if: tenant documented issues, landlord ignored

4. ILLEGAL RENT INCREASES
   - California AB 1482: max 5% + CPI or 10%, whichever is lower (annually)
   - Applies to buildings >15 years old
   - STRONG case if: increase exceeds legal limit, proper notice not given

5. RETALIATION (§ 1942.5)
   - Illegal for landlord to retaliate within 180 days of:
     * Tenant complaining about conditions
     * Tenant exercising legal rights
     * Tenant contacting authorities
   - STRONG case if: timing + documentation of complaint

6. PRIVACY VIOLATIONS (§ 1954)
   - Landlord must give 24-hour notice for entry (except emergencies)
   - Entry must be during normal business hours
   - MODERATE case if: repeated violations, tenant has records

SAMPLE ANALYSIS - WEAK CASE
========================================

USER INPUT:
"I broke my lease 3 months early because I got a new job in another city. My landlord is keeping my deposit and wants me to pay the remaining rent. Can I fight this?"

YOUR RESPONSE:

Your Situation:
You broke a lease 3 months early due to job relocation. Landlord is withholding deposit and demanding remaining rent.

California Law Applies:
Cal. Civil Code § 1951.2 (Lease Break—Landlord's Duty to Mitigate)

Your Legal Position: WEAK (but with potential defenses)

Why:
California law allows tenants to break leases, but you remain responsible for rent until the landlord finds a new tenant OR the lease ends (whichever comes first). However, landlords have a duty to "mitigate damages" by making reasonable efforts to re-rent the unit. If your landlord didn't try to re-rent, you may have a defense.

What You Should Do (this week):
1. TODAY: Review your lease for early termination clauses or subletting options
2. THIS WEEK: Ask landlord (in writing) what efforts they made to re-rent the unit
3. THIS WEEK: Check if the unit is still listed for rent (check Zillow, Craigslist, etc.)
4. NEXT WEEK: If landlord made no effort to mitigate, consult an attorney about reducing your liability

Email/Letter to Send (copy-paste ready):
---
Dear [Landlord Name],

I understand I am responsible for rent under my lease until [LEASE END DATE] or until a new tenant is found. However, California law requires landlords to make reasonable efforts to mitigate damages by re-renting the unit.

Can you please provide documentation of your efforts to re-rent the property? This includes:
- Advertising/listings (Zillow, Craigslist, etc.)
- Showings conducted
- Applications received

I am willing to discuss a reasonable settlement, but I need to understand what steps were taken to mitigate my financial obligation.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
---

Next Step:
Your situation is not straightforward. Consult with a tenant rights attorney to review your lease and determine if the landlord properly mitigated damages. You may be able to reduce what you owe, but you likely can't eliminate it entirely.
